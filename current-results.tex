\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Dataset and comparisons}
We adopt the sycophancy setting of \citet{chua_bias-augmented_2024}: a multiple-choice question is presented with and without a cue suggesting an answer. The expanded comparison includes BCT, ACT, AttCT, MLPCT, OPCT and RMCT. In the Qwen experiment represented by the provisional figures, wrong argument and suggested answer are the seen biases; distractor fact, post hoc, spurious few-shot squares and wrong few-shot are held out. LogiQA and HellaSwag form the seen datasets and are pooled; HLE text-MC is the held-out dataset.

The main comparison is data-matched. Matching requires verifying the questions and bias variants actually used by each method, including repeated exposure; equal numbers of optimisation steps need not imply equal exposure. Compute-matched comparisons, separately within activation-based and output-based methods, and convergence with validation are reported separately in the appendix. \textbf{The figures currently occupying the main-result slots use historical terminal checkpoints and do not yet implement this data-matched comparison.} They are included so that the presentation and surrounding argument can be developed while replacement results are prepared.

\subsection{Metrics}
We report the towards-bias switch rate on pairs with valid parsed answers whose clean answer differs from the promoted option. Its numerator counts pairs whose biased answer equals the promoted option. This eligibility-conditioned rate must be distinguished from the original draft's rate over all question pairs. We retain the denominator explicitly. Invalid responses are excluded from parsed-pair metrics, with missingness reported separately; they are counted as incorrect in all-generated biased accuracy.

The primary verbalisation analysis measures bias verbalisation among observed towards-bias switches with valid verbalisation grades. Overall verbalisation is reported in the appendix. Each model can have a different switched subset, so a difference between these conditional rates does not by itself identify a causal change in verbalisation on the same questions. Cue verbalisation is also distinct from monitorability: the latter asks how well a monitor can identify the specified outcome from the information made available to it.

\subsection{Does training preserve cue verbalisation and monitorability?}
Existing consistency objectives constrain responses or internal representations, including properties beyond the behaviour of interest. RMCT instead defines its consistency reward over a selected trait. We examine cue verbalisation as an outcome in its own right, and monitorability as a separate outcome.

Figure~\ref{fig:current-verbalisation} reports verbalisation on the observed towards-bias-switch subset. Overall rates and per-bias breakdowns are in the appendix. This conditional analysis focuses on responses where the sampled answer changes towards the promoted option; sampling variation means that an individual switch does not prove causal influence by the cue. The provisional observations therefore do not establish preserved faithfulness or the absence of obfuscation.

\paragraph{Monitorability.}
The Luna monitor receives reasoning only, without the clean answer or outcome label. Figure~\ref{fig:current-monitorability} reports the unfiltered available-score population: all valid scored pairs, including those whose clean answer already matches the promoted option. The target is an observed towards-bias switch, requiring that the clean answer differs from the promoted option and the biased answer matches it. Already-matched pairs are therefore non-switches. Lower-left FNR--FPR curves are better. This sampled-switch target is not causal influence ground truth. Appendix~\ref{app:monitoring} provides the verbalisation split, calibration configurations and answer-match quadrant diagnostics.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec: discussion}
The motivation for selective consistency training is to constrain the behaviour of interest without directly requiring the model to suppress mention of extraneous cues. Whether this improves the empirical trade-off between robustness, verbalisation and monitorability requires separate measurements of all three. Bias verbalisation can remain high without enabling an effective monitor, and an effective monitor can exploit correlations without identifying causal influence.

The parser audit also illustrates why the implementation of the selected trait is part of the training method. Saved-rollout replay reproduced the historical rewards and found changes under corrected final-answer extraction in both audited Qwen and Gemma training. Reparsing evaluation outputs improves the measurement of the resulting checkpoints but cannot reverse their parameter updates. The replacement comparison therefore requires training from the original pre-RMCT state with a verified parser and fresh optimiser. No claim about the direction or magnitude of the resulting performance change follows from the audit alone.

Sampling variance remains a limitation of single-pair switching measures. Repeated clean responses would help estimate a switching baseline; the available answer-transition and invariance analyses are descriptive and do not remove this confound. Our efficiency analysis likewise separates data exposure, allocated compute and validation-selected stopping rather than treating one as a proxy for another.

\section{Conclusion}
RMCT provides a method for enforcing consistency over selected behavioural properties. The revised empirical comparison asks whether this reduces conditioning on cues while preserving cue verbalisation and monitorability. The figures in this working draft establish the intended presentation; final comparative conclusions await the verified replacement experiments.
